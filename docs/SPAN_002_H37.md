## Quality on matched rows

The components are the frozen checkpoint-800 Qwen3-4B classifier, with aligned
two-order averaging, and the unchanged Gemma 26B prompted judge in its single-order
1024-token configuration. All 2,048 judge rows were scored under the repaired
current native runtime. Historical judge probabilities are not pooled into this
comparison. Model, prompt, packing and the previously fixed sample IDs are unchanged.

Five outer prompt-grouped folds evaluate component calibration and one nonnegative
scalar blend weight. Three grouped inner folds fit the weight from calibrated OOF
component predictions. The classifier uses temperature; the judge uses temperature
plus a tie intercept. Both components remain explicit controls.

| System | Nested log loss, 2,048 rows / 1,953 groups | Accuracy |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Classifier | 0.974579 | 51.90% |
| Judge | 0.986413 | 50.78% |
| Blend | **0.949572** | **56.01%** |

The blend reduces loss by **0.025007**, paired 95% group-bootstrap interval
**[0.015015, 0.035853]**, versus the classifier. Its reduction versus the judge is
0.036842, interval [0.024616, 0.048955]. Outer-fold judge weights range from
0.3926 to 0.4605. This is stronger evidence than the historical 512-row pilot,
whose 0.019308 gain had an interval including zero.

The separately frozen recipe was fitted only on the 512 re-scored controls, then
recorded before extra inference. Its judge weight is 0.4552855. Among the fixed
1,536 extras, **50 rows share a prompt group with those fit rows** and are excluded
from this check. On the remaining **1,486 rows / 1,456 groups**:

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Classifier | 0.974344 | 51.75% |

Reduction versus the classifier is **0.021022**, interval **[0.009262, 0.032468]**;

2,000 paired prompt-group bootstrap draws and condition on fitted predictions.
Development has been inspected previously; neither this check nor nested fitting

absolute losses must not be compared as model gains.

## What improves, and what still fails

On the combined cohort, weighted A/B/tie contributions to the 0.025007 gain are
**+0.015155, +0.019076, −0.009224**. Tie-class loss worsens from 0.986042 to
1.015792 and tie recall falls from 46.14% to 44.41%, although tie AUC improves
from 0.6780 to 0.6953. A tie-class slice alone does not establish that the entire
tie-probability channel became worse.

The exact three-class loss decomposition shows that **binary tie-versus-decisive
loss improves from 0.571343 to 0.567460**; its gain is 0.003882. Conditional winner
loss contributes the remaining **0.021125** improvement. Winner accuracy among
human-decisive rows rises from 68.86% to 71.69%; that is a descriptive conditional
accuracy, not the three-class accuracy or evidence of a 0.8 contest score.

This revises H43's initial rationale: preserving the classifier's tie mass may
discard a useful aggregate improvement, despite worse losses on human-tie rows.
The cache-only diagnostic stays in v3 with reduced expectations and matched
calibration controls. The deployment obstacle makes H39's budgeted cascade a more
useful next test. It must route from cheap features available before judge calls,
without consulting labels or already-computed judge predictions to choose rows.
Any improved H03 classifier must be rechecked as a new baseline; the present gains
are measured against checkpoint 800 and cannot be transferred automatically.

## Runtime, failures and provenance

The extra stage scored 1,536 rows in **1,498.845 seconds**; its internal session
time was **1,718.403 seconds**. Judge rate is 0.975810 seconds/row. Combined with
the historical classifier's measured two-order rate of 0.652394 seconds/row, the
sequential projection for an assumed 25,000 hidden rows is **49,446 seconds /
13.74 hours**, including 20% plus 600 seconds margin. This is a projection, not
measured hidden runtime, and fails this span's conservative seven-hour job guard.
No full-ensemble submission is made and no parallel-execution speedup is assumed.

Preserved execution history:

1. `/judge-expansion/1`: ERROR after one row and a 600-second server timeout.
2. `/judge-recovery/2`: native CUDA-12 libraries repaired; 32-row speed screen
   passed, historical-cache numerical identity failed; stopped without extras.
3. `/judge-consistent/2`: all 512 controls scored in 509.057 seconds, then an omitted
   import caused recipe-export failure before extra inference. Valid predictions
   were retained and the recipe was fitted/frozen locally.
4. `/judge-extra/2`: COMPLETE; only the original fixed 1,536 extras were scored.

The last three slugs share prefix `arseniytrushin/llm-preference-span-002-`.
Their version 1 saves requested no execution. Ambiguous earlier uploads were
proven absent before retries; exact source and execution receipts are retained.
Across the two retained scoring stages, source/configuration hashes, pinned CUDA
packages, T4 hardware/driver and native discovery match. The re-scored 512 differ
from historical raw probabilities by mean 0.023285 / maximum 0.304308; that drift
is disclosed and never treated as a passed identity check.
